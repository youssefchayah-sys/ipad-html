Before · During · After

How to keep a country

You don't forget places because your memory is weak — you forget them because the experiences arrived as beautiful, disconnected fragments with nothing to stick to. The fix is to give every country a skeleton before you arrive, hang the trip on it while you're there, and revisit it after. Learn less per country, but deeply — and it lasts for life.

Before · ~2 hrs

Build the skeleton

Consume one good narrative — a single accessible history, a documentary, or a serious podcast series. Then answer seven fixed lenses on one page: Founding Story · Central Tension · Geography Logic · Economic Engine · The People · Five Hinge Moments · The Emblematic Place & its real meaning. The lenses never change — that's what makes it a habit.

During · kid-proof

Three tiny habits

One surprise a day: a 60-second voice memo — "the one thing that surprised me today was…". The dinner debrief: tell Salma the single thing you understood that day — explaining out loud is the strongest memory tool there is. Photograph the plaque, not the view — and look with your own eyes for 30 seconds before lifting the phone.

After · the step everyone skips

Debrief & space it out

Within a week, turn the voice memos into one page: not a diary, but "here's what I now understand about this country." Pick your three marvels — the most dinner-worthy facts, written as tiny stories. Then revisit at 1 week, 1 month, 6 months — five minutes each. That spacing is the entire difference between a memory that lasts a fortnight and one that lasts decades.

The anchor trick

Hook new facts to old loves

A new fact sticks when it latches onto something you already hold dear. Singapore's independence day is 9 August — Emeer's birthday. Berlin is where you met Salma. Your honeymoon was Thailand. Each personal anchor below is a permanent hook the national story can hang from.

Japan

Tokyo · Kyoto · Osaka · Kobe
Personal anchorTravelled with Salma & Emeer. The Kobe you walked rebuilt itself from the 1995 earthquake within your own lifetime.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

Tradition versus modernity, held in permanent, deliberate balance — a 1,200-year-old shrine beside a robot café, and neither apologising.

The People & Demography

  • ~98% ethnically Japanese — strikingly homogeneous.
  • Most Japanese are both Shinto and Buddhist without contradiction: 'born Shinto, die Buddhist'.
  • A rapidly ageing, shrinking population — the quiet anxiety under modern Japan.

Geography Logic

  • Archipelago on the Pacific Ring of Fire — ~¾ mountains, almost no natural resources.
  • Population crammed onto narrow coastal plains → Tokyo's extreme density.
  • Must import raw materials, export finished goods. Scarcity + earthquakes bred precision.

History — five hinges

  • 1603 — Tokugawa shogunate; 250 yrs sealed isolation (Edo period).
  • 1853 — Perry's 'Black Ships' force the country open at gunpoint.
  • 1868 — Meiji Restoration; capital moves Kyoto→Edo, renamed Tokyo ('Eastern Capital').
  • 1945 — Defeat, Hiroshima/Nagasaki, total reconstruction.
  • 1995 — Kobe earthquake (you were there): 6,400+ dead, a symbolic rebuild.

The Economy

  • Postwar 'miracle': from 1945 ruin to world's #2 economy by the 1980s on cars, electronics, precision machinery.
  • ~1990 bubble burst → the 'Lost Decades' of deflation and stagnation.
  • At the bubble's peak, land under the Imperial Palace was theoretically worth more than all of California.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • Pre-2011: ~30% nuclear. After Fukushima, reactors shut → Japan became the world's largest LNG importer.
  • Famous grid quirk: east runs 50 Hz, west 60 Hz — a legacy of buying German vs American generators in the 1890s. The two halves still can't freely share power.
  • Now restarting nuclear (Kashiwazaki-Kariwa, Sendai) and racing into offshore wind. The Shinkansen (1964) is still the world's benchmark high-speed rail.
Project-finance note: Japan's mega-banks (MUFG, SMBC, Mizuho) are perennially among the world's top project-finance lenders — Japan exports capital and EPC to global infra, not just goods.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • Kyoto's wooden temples exist only because the US Secretary of War personally struck the city from the atomic-target list.
  • The orange torii gates = Shinto shrines; the temples = Buddhist. Two religions, comfortably layered.
  • Ise Grand Shrine is torn down and rebuilt identically every 20 years — both 1,300 years old and a few years old.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. The temples I walked through in Kyoto only still exist because one American official took the city off the atomic-bomb target list.
  2. There's a shrine that's both 1,300 years old and a few years old — they demolish and rebuild it identically every 20 years, and have for over a millennium.
  3. Japan's electricity grid is split in two — east on 50 Hz, west on 60 Hz — because in the 1890s Tokyo bought German generators and Osaka bought American ones, and they never reconciled.

Singapore

The City-State
Personal anchorIndependence day is 9 August 1965 — Emeer's birthday. Singapore's National Day is your son's.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

The survival of a tiny, resource-less, multi-ethnic city-state — answered through order, meritocracy, and total openness to trade. Order versus freedom.

The People & Demography

  • ~¾ Chinese, with significant Malay and Indian populations.
  • Four official languages; English chosen as the unifier so no ethnic group 'won'.
  • One of the densest, most urbanised societies on Earth — yet engineered green.

Geography Logic

  • Sits on the Strait of Malacca — one of the planet's great shipping chokepoints.
  • No farmland, no fresh water, no resources. Its strategy was to monetise its location.
  • Geography is the economy: become the indispensable port and hub.

History — the founding

  • Expelled from Malaysia → reluctant independence, 9 Aug 1965.
  • Lee Kuan Yew wept on live TV — a city with nothing, surrounded by giants.
  • 'From Third World to First' in a single generation.

The Economy

  • Transshipment port, finance, refining, high-end manufacturing.
  • One of the world's busiest container ports; a top global financial centre.
  • Famously low corruption, high-salary clean government — by deliberate design.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • NEWater: purified recycled wastewater clean enough to drink — water security as national survival, because depending on Malaysia (the neighbour that expelled it) was intolerable.
  • No domestic fuel → among Asia's first LNG import terminals; Jurong Island is a giant engineered petrochemical hub.
  • Now importing clean power across borders: solar from Australia (Sun Cable subsea HVDC) and hydro from Laos — building a regional grid out of a city with no land.
Project-finance note: Singapore is Asia's project-finance command centre — the legal seat, the arrangers, and the capital for cross-border energy & infra deals across Southeast Asia.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • Gardens by the Bay isn't decoration — it's LKY's 'Garden City' doctrine made physical: greening as social policy AND an investor signal.
  • The 1992 chewing-gum ban is the same idea in miniature: order as identity.
  • Marina Barrage turned the harbour into a freshwater reservoir — water-security as a tourist attraction.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. Singapore drinks its own recycled sewage on purpose, and it's some of the safest water on Earth — that's the whole country in one sip.
  2. On the day it became independent, its founding father cried on national television, because being free terrified him more than being ruled.
  3. It's importing solar electricity from Australia through a cable laid across the bottom of the sea — a country with no land buying sunshine from another continent.

Greece

Santorini · the Aegean
Personal anchorThe crescent you admired across the Aegean is a drowned volcano — the catastrophe IS the view.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

The birthplace of Western civilisation, now living among — and on — its own ruins. Glory and decline, side by side.

The People & Demography

  • ~10–11 million, deeply tied to the Orthodox Church and to family/island identity.
  • A vast diaspora — more 'Greeks' live abroad than the homeland sometimes feels.
  • Ageing population and a brain drain after the debt crisis.

Geography Logic

  • ~6,000 islands (a couple hundred inhabited) → a maritime, fragmented nation.
  • Mountains and sea, little arable land → seafaring, trade, shipping in the DNA.
  • Santorini = the rim of a caldera; the lagoon is the collapsed crater.

History — the hinges

  • ~1600 BC — Minoan (Thera) eruption, one of the largest in human history; may seed the Atlantis myth.
  • 5th c. BC — Athens: democracy, philosophy, drama.
  • 1453 — Constantinople falls; centuries under Ottoman rule.
  • 1821 — War of Independence.
  • 2009–18 — sovereign debt crisis and brutal austerity.

The Economy

  • Shipping (Greeks control a huge share of the world's merchant tonnage) and tourism are the twin engines.
  • The 2010s debt crisis nearly broke the eurozone.
  • Now recovering — energy and tourism led.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • The islands' great challenge: each was historically a stand-alone diesel grid. The Cyclades and Crete interconnectors are huge undersea-cable projects stitching them to the mainland.
  • The GSI / 'Great Sea Interconnector' aims to link the grids of Israel, Cyprus and Greece — one of the longest, deepest power cables ever attempted.
  • Rapid solar/wind growth; phasing out heavily-polluting lignite.
Project-finance note: Post-crisis privatisations (the grid operator ADMIE, ports, airports) and EU-funded island interconnectors made Greece a live infrastructure-finance market again.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • Santorini's white villages perch on the lip of a volcano that destroyed a Bronze-Age civilisation.
  • The dramatic black/red beaches are volcanic ash and lava.
  • Akrotiri — a Minoan 'Pompeii' frozen under ash, only partly excavated.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. The postcard view of Santorini is actually the inside of a volcano — you're standing on the rim of a crater that erupted so violently 3,600 years ago it may have erased the civilisation that inspired the legend of Atlantis.
  2. Greek shipowners, from a country of 10 million, control one of the largest merchant fleets on the planet.
  3. Greece is now laying one of the deepest electricity cables ever built across the seabed to wire the Middle East into Europe's grid.

Spain

Barcelona · Granada
Personal anchorIn Granada's Alhambra you stood at the very end of Al-Andalus — Muslim Spain's last kingdom, fallen in 1492.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

A 'nation of nations' — Catalan and Basque identities straining against Madrid — layered over an 800-year Muslim–Christian story.

The People & Demography

  • Several nations in one state: Castilian, Catalan, Basque, Galician — distinct languages and identities.
  • Catalonia's independence movement is the live wire of modern Spanish politics.
  • Among the world's longest life expectancies; a steep birth-rate decline.

Geography Logic

  • A high central plateau (the Meseta) ringed by mountains → historically fragmented regions.
  • The Pyrenees nearly cut Spain off from France — geographically an 'island' of Europe.
  • Strait of Gibraltar: 14 km from Africa, the bridge for Al-Andalus.

History — the hinges

  • 711 — Muslim conquest; Al-Andalus, a centre of learning for centuries.
  • 1492 — Granada falls (end of Reconquista) + Columbus sails + Jews expelled, all one year.
  • 1936–39 — Civil War; then Franco's dictatorship to 1975.
  • 1978 — democracy and a decentralised constitution.
  • 2017 — Catalan independence referendum crisis.

The Economy

  • Tourism superpower, strong agriculture (olive oil, wine), autos, and renewables.
  • Boom–bust property cycle; hammered in 2008, since recovered.
  • Persistent high youth unemployment.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • A renewables leader — vast solar and wind — but historically an 'energy island', barely interconnected to France across the Pyrenees.
  • That isolation turned real on 28 April 2025, when Spain & Portugal suffered a massive Iberian blackout — a live lesson in grid resilience.
  • Key gateway for North-African gas: the Medgaz pipeline runs from Algeria — and the broader Maghreb energy web touches your Tunisia base.
Project-finance note: Spanish developers (Iberdrola, Acciona, ACS) are global renewables & infra champions — Spain exports project-finance expertise worldwide, especially in solar and toll roads.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • The Alhambra: a Moorish palace-fortress whose water gardens were paradise-engineering in a dry land.
  • Barcelona's Sagrada Família — Gaudí's still-unfinished cathedral, begun 1882.
  • Córdoba's Mezquita: a mosque with a cathedral built inside it — Spain's whole history in one building.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. In Granada you can walk through the last palace of Muslim Spain, surrendered in 1492 — the same year Columbus sailed and the same year the Jews were expelled. Three world-changing events, one year, one country.
  2. Spain generates so much solar and wind it sometimes can't get rid of it — because the Pyrenees leave it barely connected to the rest of Europe's grid, an 'energy island' that blacked out an entire peninsula in 2025.
  3. The Sagrada Família has been under construction for over 140 years and still isn't finished — the architect said his client (God) was 'in no hurry'.

Germany

Berlin · the Fernsehturm
Personal anchor — the big oneBerlin is where you met Salma — your neighbour from Monastir — while she studied German. The Fernsehturm you climbed was an East German prestige tower, built to loom over a divided city. You met under the skyline of a once-split Berlin.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

A nation built atop its own moral catastrophe, in permanent reckoning — and a country split in two for 40 years, still healing the seam.

The People & Demography

  • ~84 million — the EU's most populous nation; an economic anchor of Europe.
  • Ageing fast → relies heavily on immigration; took in ~1M+ refugees in 2015.
  • Still a faint East/West divide in wages, politics and outlook.

Geography Logic

  • Flat, open central-European corridor — no natural defensive borders → history of invasion and being invaded.
  • The Rhine and great rivers made it an industrial heartland.
  • A unified nation only since 1871 — late, and consequential.

History — the hinges

  • 1871 — unification under Prussia.
  • 1933–45 — Nazism, the Holocaust, WWII.
  • 1945 — defeat; the country split into West (FRG) and East (GDR).
  • 1961 — Berlin Wall built; 1989 — Wall falls.
  • 1990 — reunification.

The Economy

  • The 'Mittelstand' — world-beating mid-sized engineering firms — plus cars and machinery.
  • Export powerhouse; long ran enormous trade surpluses.
  • Now squeezed by high energy costs and Chinese competition in autos.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • The 'Energiewende' — the great energy transition: a massive bet on renewables AND a full nuclear phase-out (completed April 2023).
  • For decades it ran on cheap Russian gas via Nord Stream — until 2022, when that ended overnight and Germany scrambled to build LNG import terminals in record time.
  • One of the world's largest, most-watched energy experiments — and a cautionary tale about closing nuclear before renewables are ready.
Project-finance note: Germany's KfW is a model state development bank; its energy transition created one of the deepest renewable project-finance markets on Earth. Watch the new LNG-terminal and grid-expansion pipeline.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • The Fernsehturm (1969): East Germany's tallest structure, built so the GDR could literally tower over the West — a sphere whose reflected cross the regime couldn't erase ('the Pope's Revenge').
  • The Brandenburg Gate stood inside the Wall's death strip — now the symbol of reunification.
  • The Holocaust Memorial near it: a nation choosing to put its worst sin at its centre, not hide it.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. The TV tower I stood under was built by communist East Germany to dominate the skyline of the West — but when the sun hits its steel sphere it throws a giant cross, which religious East Germans gleefully nicknamed 'the Pope's Revenge'.
  2. Germany ran its whole economy on cheap Russian gas through a pipeline under the Baltic — until 2022, when it had to replace that overnight and build floating gas terminals faster than anyone thought possible.
  3. This is the only country I know that put a monument to its own greatest crime in the dead centre of its capital, on purpose — a nation that decided remembering its worst was the point.

Thailand

Phuket · the South
Personal anchorYour honeymoon with Salma. The name 'Thailand' literally means 'land of the free'.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

The only Southeast-Asian nation never colonised — balancing a revered monarchy, deep Buddhism, and a tourism-dependent economy.

The People & Demography

  • ~70 million, overwhelmingly Theravada Buddhist.
  • A Thai-Chinese commercial elite is woven through the economy.
  • The monarchy is constitutionally protected by strict lèse-majesté laws — criticising the king is illegal.

Geography Logic

  • Sits between the Indian Ocean and the Pacific worlds — a crossroads.
  • The fertile central plain (the Chao Phraya rice basin) is the historic heart; the south is islands and tourism.
  • Stayed independent partly by being a buffer between British Burma and French Indochina.

History — the hinges

  • Never formally colonised — uniquely in the region.
  • 1932 — absolute monarchy becomes constitutional.
  • Long cycle of military coups and elected governments.
  • 2004 — the Indian Ocean tsunami devastated the Andaman coast (incl. Phuket).

The Economy

  • Tourism, electronics, autos ('the Detroit of Asia'), and agriculture (rice, rubber).
  • Pioneered Asia's IPP (Independent Power Producer) model for private electricity.
  • Vulnerable to tourism shocks — as the pandemic brutally showed.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • Powered largely by natural gas from the Gulf of Thailand, supplemented by hydro imported from Laos ('the battery of Southeast Asia').
  • An early regional leader in opening power generation to private investors — a template much of Asia copied.
  • Building out a regional grid and LNG imports as domestic gas declines.
Project-finance note: Thailand's 1990s IPP programme was foundational for Asian power project finance — and Lao hydro exported to Thailand is a textbook cross-border PPA structure.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • Glittering wat (temples) and the Grand Palace — power and faith fused.
  • Phuket / Phi Phi: limestone karst islands rising sheer from turquoise sea.
  • Floating markets — commerce on water, the old river-based life.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. Thailand is the one country in Southeast Asia the Europeans never colonised — it survived by playing the British and the French off each other as a buffer state, and its name literally means 'land of the free'.
  2. Much of Thailand's electricity flows in from Laos, a tiny mountainous neighbour that calls itself 'the battery of Southeast Asia' and sells its rivers' power across the border.
  3. It is illegal to insult the Thai king — even a comment, even online — under some of the strictest royal-protection laws on Earth.

United Kingdom — London

London
Personal anchorThe capital where a quarter of the planet was once run from a few square miles of riverside.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

A small island that once governed a quarter of humanity — now renegotiating its identity post-empire and post-Brexit, as a global money capital.

The People & Demography

  • One of the world's most diverse cities — ~300 languages spoken in London.
  • Powerful North–South economic divide within the UK.
  • Brexit (2016) exposed deep splits over identity and openness.

Geography Logic

  • The Thames made London a port at the lowest bridgeable point — trade by water.
  • An island nation → a navy, then an empire, then global finance.
  • Small, crowded, temperate; food and energy heavily imported.

History — the hinges

  • 1066 — Norman Conquest.
  • 1707 — Union with Scotland creates 'Great Britain'.
  • 19th c. — the Empire at its zenith; London the world's financial centre.
  • 1945 — exhausted victor; empire unwinds.
  • 2016/2020 — Brexit referendum and departure from the EU.

The Economy

  • The City of London — finance, insurance, legal services — is the engine.
  • Services-dominated; manufacturing long in relative decline.
  • Post-Brexit friction with its largest market, the EU.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • A world leader in offshore wind — Dogger Bank in the North Sea is among the largest wind projects ever built.
  • North Sea oil & gas, once a windfall, now in long decline.
  • Hinkley Point C — a giant new nuclear plant built by France's EDF (with Chinese capital) — a landmark, and notoriously expensive, project-finance saga. Plus the Thames Tideway 'super sewer' beneath London.
Project-finance note: The City of London is the world's premier project-finance arrangement hub — UK 'PFI' pioneered (for better and worse) the private financing of public infrastructure now used globally.

The Emblematic Place, decoded

  • The City's 'Square Mile' — Roman in origin, still the financial heart.
  • Westminster & the Houses of Parliament — the 'mother of parliaments'.
  • Greenwich — where world time and longitude are measured from (0°).

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. From a single square mile by a river, Britain once governed roughly a quarter of all the people on Earth — and that same square mile is still one of the two or three places that runs the world's money.
  2. The line that divides East from West on every map and clock on the planet — zero longitude — runs through a London suburb, because Britain was powerful enough to make the world measure time from its own backyard.
  3. Britain's new flagship nuclear plant is being built by the French and was part-funded by China — a neat picture of a post-empire nation that now imports the capacity to build its own future.

United Kingdom — Scotland

Edinburgh
Personal anchorEdinburgh — the small city that helped invent the modern mind: economics, philosophy, the Enlightenment.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

A proud nation inside a union it keeps questioning — and the city that powered the Enlightenment that built the modern world.

The People & Demography

  • ~5.5 million, with a fierce distinct identity within the UK.
  • The 2014 independence referendum (No, 55–45) still shapes everything.
  • Strong cultural exports far beyond its size — and a vast global Scottish diaspora.

Geography Logic

Italy

Milano · Lago di Como · Bellagio · Varenna
Personal anchor — a family caravanA January trip with Salma, little Emeer and baby Eline, plus Salma's family — her mother Wima, brother Degué and sister Sasa. Milano's centro storico and the Castello Sforzesco, then the villa-lined arms of Lago di Como (Bellagio, Varenna): a wooden boat at dawn and you at the helm by afternoon, an Italian singalong playing the whole way.
The Central Tension — the one line to remember

A country that lived for most of history as rival city-states and foreign-ruled kingdoms — united only in 1861 — and still feels like a thousand Italies: the industrial North against the sunlit South, world-class 'Made in Italy' genius against chronic political churn.

The People & Demography

  • ~59 million — among the world's oldest populations and lowest birth rates; a demographic slow-burn.
  • Fierce local identity: campanilismo — loyalty to your home town's bell tower — plus dozens of living dialects.
  • Historically Catholic (the Vatican sits inside Rome); family is the true unit of society.

Geography Logic

  • A boot-shaped peninsula thrust into the Mediterranean → a maritime, trading, endlessly-invaded crossroads.
  • The Alps wall off the north; the Apennines run down the spine; the south is volcanic (Vesuvio, Etna).
  • Lago di Como is a glacier-carved lake shaped like an upside-down 'Y', among Europe's deepest (~410 m) — its walls so steep the villages cling to the water.

History — the hinges

  • Ancient Rome — and Milan (Mediolanum) served as a capital of the Western Roman Empire (~286–402 AD).
  • 313 AD — the Edict of Milan (Editto di Milano) legalises Christianity across the empire.
  • Rinascimento — Leonardo paints the Last Supper in Milan under the Sforza dukes.
  • Centuries of fragmentation and foreign rule (Spanish, Austrian, French).
  • 1861 — Unification (Risorgimento); 1946 — Republic; 1950s–60s — il boom, the economic miracle.

The Economy

  • The eurozone's 3rd-largest economy; a manufacturing & design powerhouse — Made in Italy (fashion, food, machinery, Ferrari & Lamborghini).
  • Milano is the financial & fashion capital — the Borsa (stock exchange) and the Quadrilatero della Moda.
  • Persistent drags: high public debt and the North–South gap (il Mezzogiorno).

Energy & Infrastructure

  • Italy abandoned nuclear power by referendum after Chernobyl (1987) — one of the only G7 states with zero reactors — and is now openly debating a return.
  • After 2022 it pivoted off Russian gas onto North Africa: the TransMed / 'Enrico Mattei' pipeline carries Algerian gas across Tunisia and under the Sicilian Channel.
  • Enel is among the world's biggest utilities and renewable developers; Eni a global major; Italo was Europe's first private open-access high-speed-rail operator.
Project-finance note — your world: The TransMed line lands Algerian gas via Tunisia — energy infrastructure threading straight through your second home. Enel & Eni make Italy a global energy project-finance heavyweight; Rome's 'Piano Mattei' aims to make the country Europe's southern energy gateway.

Milano, decoded

  • The Duomo (Gothic cathedral) took nearly 600 years (1386–1965); walk the rooftop terrazze among 135 spires, crowned by the golden Madonnina.
  • Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II (1877) — 'il salotto di Milano', Milan's drawing room; spin your heel on the mosaic bull for luck.
  • Castello Sforzesco — the Sforza dukes' fortress; inside, Michelangelo's final unfinished Pietà Rondanini; behind it, Parco Sempione.
  • Leonardo's L'Ultima Cena (The Last Supper) hides in Santa Maria delle Grazie — book months ahead.

Lago di Como, decoded

  • Bellagio — 'la Perla del Lario', the Pearl of the Lake — sits exactly where the lake's two arms fork.
  • The great villas were aristocratic pleasure-estates: Villa del Balbianello (film set for Star Wars and James Bond's Casino Royale, and the fashion drama House of Gucci), Villa Carlotta, Villa Melzi, Villa Monastero at Varenna.
  • Alessandro Volta — inventor of the battery, namesake of the 'volt' — was born in Como (see the Tempio Voltiano).
  • Como is Italy's historic silk capital — la città della seta — and Varenna hides the Passeggiata degli Innamorati (Lovers' Walk).

A Tavola — the table

  • Milano: risotto alla milanese (saffron-gold risotto), cotoletta alla milanese (breaded veal cutlet), ossobuco (braised veal shank with gremolata), and panettone — the Christmas cake born here.
  • Lago di Como: missoltini (sun-dried lake shad) with polenta, and risotto al pesce persico (perch risotto).
  • Bresaola — Sasa's discovery — is the air-dried salted beef of the Valtellina, the alpine valley just north of the lake; sliced paper-thin with lemon and olive oil, it's the region on a plate (and the perfect travelling snack).
  • Aperitivo is a Milanese ritual — Campari was invented here; ask for a Negroni Sbagliato, created by accident at Milan's Bar Basso.

Three marvels — to deploy at dinner

Surprising fact · your moment · the big meaning
  1. Milan is the fashion capital now — but it was once a capital of the Roman Empire, and in 313 AD the Edict of Milan legalised Christianity across the Western world. The city of the catwalk once changed the religion of the West.
  2. The word 'volt' is a souvenir from that lake — Alessandro Volta, who built the first battery, was born in Como. Every time you charge a phone you're using a unit named after a man from the shore we swam off.
  3. Milan's cathedral took almost six hundred years to build — begun in 1386, before Columbus sailed, finished only in 1965 — and you can walk across its roof through a forest of 135 marble spires.

Japan

Tradition versus modernity, held in permanent, deliberate balance — a 1,200-year-old shrine beside a robot café, and neither apologising.

The People & Demography

  • ~98% ethnically Japanese — strikingly homogeneous.
  • Most Japanese are both Shinto and Buddhist without contradiction: 'born Shinto, die Buddhist'.
  • A rapidly ageing, shrinking population — the quiet anxiety under modern Japan.

Geography Logic

  • Archipelago on the Pacific Ring of Fire — ~¾ mountains, almost no natural resources.
  • Population crammed onto narrow coastal plains → Tokyo's extreme density.
  • Must import raw materials, export finished goods. Scarcity + earthquakes bred precision.

History — five hinges

  • 1603 — Tokugawa shogunate; 250 yrs sealed isolation (Edo period).
  • 1853 — Perry's 'Black Ships' force the country open at gunpoint.
  • 1868 — Meiji Restoration; capital moves Kyoto→Edo, renamed Tokyo ('Eastern Capital').
  • 1945 — Defeat, Hiroshima/Nagasaki, total reconstruction.
  • 1995 — Kobe earthquake (you were there): 6,400+ dead, a symbolic rebuild.

The Economy

  • Postwar 'miracle': from 1945 ruin to world's #2 economy by the 1980s on cars, electronics, precision machinery.
  • ~1990 bubble burst → the 'Lost Decades' of deflation and stagnation.
  • At the bubble's peak, land under the Imperial Palace was theoretically worth more than all of California.

Energy & Infrastructure

  • Pre-2011: ~30% nuclear. After Fukushima, reactors shut → Japan became the world's largest LNG importer.

  2. There's a shrine that's both 1,300 years old and a few years old — they demolish and rebuild it identically every 20 years, and have for over a millennium.

                                                • Flat, forested, on the Baltic Sea — an open invasion corridor with no natural defences.

                                                • On the fault line between Russian and Western spheres — geography as destiny.

                                                • 1918 — independence after WWI.
                                                • 1940 — annexed by the USSR (Hitler–Stalin pact); then Nazi, then Soviet again.

                                                • 2004 — joined EU & NATO.

                                                • Fast-growing, digital, EU-integrated 'Baltic tigers'.
                                                • Estonia is a global e-governance pioneer — e-residency, online voting, near-paperless state.
                                                • Heavy focus on cutting all economic dependence on Russia.

                                                • THE story: in February 2025 the three Baltics cut themselves off from the Russian/Belarus power grid (the old 'BRELL' ring) and synchronised with continental Europe — a landmark geopolitical infrastructure project, ending a Soviet-era dependence.

                                                • Rail Baltica: a huge EU-funded high-speed line finally linking the Baltics into Europe's rail network.

                                                3. Estonia runs almost its entire government online — you can vote, sign legal documents and start a company from your phone — and it became the world's most digital state precisely because a small country has to be efficient to survive.

                                                          The Strategy Desk

                                                          From Scarcity to Power

                                                          A different lens for a different question. Not "what is this place like?" but "how did it climb?" — and, harder, "why did it stay up when others slid back?" The filter here is deliberate: we exclude nations that struck rich on one commodity, cheap labour, or a lucky geopolitical hand. What remains is the rarer breed — economies that engineered durable prosperity, and survived the storms that exposed the pretenders.

                                                          The repeatable recipe — the head of the snake

                                                          Strong institutions · world-class education · relentless innovation · ruthless diversification · fiscal discipline · openness to global trade. The commodity windfall is a sugar high; this is the metabolism that keeps a nation rich for generations.

                                                          Diversified, institutionally deep, crisis-tested. The cleanest proof of the recipe.

                                                          Singapore

                                                          Tier 1
                                                          From an expelled, water-less swamp-port to a first-world command centre in one lifetime — the purest proof that strategy can beat geography.
                                                          Demographics

                                                          ~5.9M, ~¾ Chinese with Malay & Indian minorities; English as the neutral unifier; one of the densest, most educated populations on Earth.

                                                          Economy

                                                          High-income; finance, transshipment, refining, biotech & high-end manufacturing. Two sovereign wealth funds (GIC, Temasek) bank the surplus for the lean years.

                                                          Politics

                                                          Dominant-party state (PAP) since 1959 — soft-authoritarian, technocratic, near-zero corruption by design. Order purchased with prosperity.

                                                          Brief history

                                                          A British trading post, briefly part of Malaysia, expelled in 1965 with no resources and no hinterland. Lee Kuan Yew turned vulnerability into doctrine.

                                                          The breakthrough — how, when, what · 1965 → 1990s

                                                          Bet everything on its location and its people: pro-business rule of law, English-language schooling, anti-corruption courts, and aggressive courting of multinationals — manufacturing first, then finance. 'From Third World to First' in 25 years.

                                                          • Institutions and education are the moat, not the minerals.

                                                          Consensual welfare democracy built on 'flexicurity' — easy hiring and firing paired with a strong safety net. Flexibility and security, not a trade-off.

                                                          Brief history

                                                          A former agricultural and maritime nation that reinvented itself around design, pharma and renewables.

                                                          The breakthrough — how, when, what · late 20th c. → today

                                                          Bet early and hard on wind power (decades before it was fashionable) and on high-value pharma and logistics — turning a small domestic market into global category leadership in a handful of industries.

                                                          Lessons learned
                                                          • Win globally in a few niches rather than competing everywhere.
                                                          • First-mover commitment to a transition (wind) becomes a durable export industry.
                                                          • 'Flexicurity' shows a safety net and a dynamic labour market can coexist.

                                                          Extraordinary rises whose long-run resilience is still being written.

                                                          China

                                                          Tier 3
                                                          The largest poverty-to-power leap in human history — ~800 million lifted out of poverty — but resilience is now the open question, not the settled fact.
                                                          Demographics

                                                          ~1.4B and now shrinking & ageing fast — the one-child legacy is the demographic cliff under the growth story.

                                                          Economy

                                                          Manufacturing and infrastructure colossus moving up into EVs, solar, AI and chips — while wrestling a vast property overhang and heavy local-government debt.

                                                          Politics

                                                          One-party state-capitalist model; sweeping central control, with the trade-off of opacity and concentrated political risk.

                                                          Brief history

                                                          Mao-era poverty and upheaval, then Deng Xiaoping's 1978 'reform and opening' unleashed four decades of double-digit growth.

                                                          The breakthrough — how, when, what · 1978 → 2010s

                                                          Deng opened the economy to markets and the world: special economic zones, export manufacturing, and state-directed infrastructure on a scale never attempted — the 'factory of the world'.

                                                          Lessons learned
                                                          • Scale and state direction can industrialise at unmatched speed.
                                                          • But growth on debt, property and demography is harder to sustain than to start.
                                                          • Less obviously resilient than Singapore or Switzerland — the institutions are powerful but untested by a genuine succession or downturn.

                                                          United Arab Emirates

                                                          Tier 3
                                                          The Gulf's most convincing escape from the oil trap — racing to build a post-hydrocarbon economy before the hydrocarbons run out. Relevant to your own horizon.
                                                          Demographics

                                                          ~10M, but ~85–90% expatriate — a workforce imported, not grown. The deepest question mark over long-term resilience.

                                                          Economy

                                                          Diversifying hard: aviation (Emirates), logistics (DP World, Jebel Ali), tourism, real estate and financial services (DIFC). Oil's share of GDP keeps falling.

                                                          Politics

                                                          Federation of seven emirates under hereditary rule; stable, business-first, top-down. Speed of execution is the system's signature.

                                                          Brief history

                                                          A cluster of pearl-diving and trading sheikhdoms, transformed by oil from the 1960s, federated in 1971, and deliberately pivoting beyond oil since the 2000s.

                                                          The breakthrough — how, when, what · 2000s → today

                                                          Used oil wealth as seed capital — not a hammock — to build world-class aviation, ports, free zones and tourism, positioning Dubai and Abu Dhabi as global hubs rather than mere petro-states.

                                                          Lessons learned
                                                          • The smart use of a windfall is to fund the thing that replaces it.
                                                          • Diversification away from the commodity is the whole game for a resource state.
                                                          • Unproven over generations: an expat-built, young economy still has to show it can endure a real downturn and a leadership transition.

                                                          The Cautionary Wing — rich once, resilient never

                                                          These reached the summit but couldn't hold the altitude — proof that wealth and resilience are not the same animal. The common thread: prosperity rented from a resource or a moment, not built into the institutions.
                                                          🇻🇪 Venezuela — World's largest oil reserves, yet economic collapse — commodity dependence with weak institutions.
                                                          🇳🇬 Nigeria — Africa's giant, but growth tethered to oil; diversification has lagged.
                                                          🇷🇺 Russia — A resource state — oil, gas, metals — wealthy in cycles, structurally exposed.
                                                          🇶🇦 Qatar — Spectacularly rich, but still overwhelmingly hydrocarbon-based; the diversification is young.
                                                          🇦🇷 Argentina — Was among the world's richest c.1900 — then a century of repeated macro instability. The cautionary tale that wealth can be lost.
